# Supplementary Material

## Educational video game to support food safety knowledge, attitudes and practices among gastronomy students

*CIIS 2026 — methodological transparency and reproducibility documentation*

**Purpose.** This repository contains the supplementary material associated with the CIIS 2026 manuscript. It documents the systematic-review corpus, the KAP source instrument and study adaptation, the controlled-experiment protocol, the deterministic difficulty-adjustment procedure, and the usability/player-experience pilot results referenced in the manuscript.

# S1. Systematic literature review documentation

A systematic literature review was conducted in Scopus and Web of Science to identify peer-reviewed empirical studies published between 2019 and 2026. The search strategy combined four groups of concepts: population, educational intervention, technical or regulatory context, and evaluated outcomes. The Boolean search string was:

```text
("higher education" OR "university students" OR "professional training" OR "vocational education") AND ("serious game*" OR "educational video game*" OR "game-based learning") AND ("technical training" OR "regulatory training" OR procedure* OR compliance OR "food safety") AND ("learning outcome*" OR knowledge OR attitude* OR practice* OR usability OR "player experience")
```

Studies were included when a serious or educational game constituted a central component of learning in a technical, professional, or regulatory context and the study reported empirical learning, performance, usability, or player-experience outcomes. Reviews, non-empirical papers, studies conducted exclusively with children, entertainment-only games, papers without full-text access, and studies without relevant educational or user-experience outcomes were excluded. Following the screening process, 32 empirical studies met the eligibility criteria and constituted the corpus synthesized in the systematic review.

# S2. KAP instrument documentation

Food-safety knowledge, attitudes, and self-reported practices were assessed using items adapted from Akabanda et al. (2017). The original questionnaire was peer-reviewed and pilot-tested in their study and comprises 25 knowledge items, 20 attitude items, and 11 self-reported practice items. Knowledge items address personal hygiene, cross-contamination, foodborne diseases, microorganisms, temperature control, storage, and hygienic handling. The source article is distributed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License (CC BY 4.0).

Two parallel forms were used for the pretest and posttest. Each form contained 10 knowledge items, 10 attitude items, and 5 self-reported practice items. Items were distributed to provide comparable coverage of the food-safety domains addressed by both the educational game and the control material, including personal hygiene, contamination prevention, cleaning and sanitation, temperature control, storage and receiving, food-handler health, and safe operational practices. Each item was scored dichotomously: a correct response received 1 point, whereas an incorrect or “Do not know / remember” response received 0 points. For self-reported practices, responses were keyed so that the safer food-handling response received 1 point and the non-compliant response received 0 points. Dimension scores therefore ranged from 0 to 10 for knowledge, 0 to 10 for attitudes, and 0 to 5 for practices; higher scores represented stronger food-safety knowledge, more favorable attitudes toward compliance, and safer self-reported practices.

# S3. Controlled-experiment protocol

Step-by-step procedure followed to recruit, allocate, expose, measure, and analyze participants:

| **Step** | **Stage**                 | **Procedure**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
|----------|---------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1        | Recruitment               | Thirty-two volunteer fourth-cycle gastronomy students aged 18–22 years were recruited from a private university in Lima. The sample comprised 15 women and 17 men. Participants reported no prior kitchen-work experience or previous food-safety training.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 2        | Consent and coding        | Written informed consent was obtained before participation. Anonymous participant codes were assigned, and participants were informed about confidentiality, voluntary participation, their right to withdraw, and responsible data handling.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 3        | Random allocation         | A web-based random group generator was used to allocate the 32 participants to the experimental group (n = 16) and control group (n = 16). No stratification criteria were applied during allocation.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 4        | Pretest                   | Both groups completed the digital KAP pretest before the intervention.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 5        | Experimental orientation  | Before gameplay, participants in the experimental group received a short tutorial explaining the controls, game flow, and study objective. They were allowed to request procedural assistance during the session.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| 6        | Experimental intervention | Each participant in the experimental group completed one individual 40-minute session with the adaptive web-based educational video game. Sessions were scheduled within a one-week intervention period.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| 7        | Control condition         | During the same intervention period, the control group completed a 40-minute passive-slide review covering equivalent food-safety content, including hygiene management, separation of foods by risk, temperature control, receiving, storage, preparation, operational cleanliness, and verification of sanitary conditions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 8        | Posttest                  | Both groups completed the digital KAP posttest after the intervention period.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 9        | Statistical analysis      | For each participant and KAP dimension, the change score was calculated as posttest minus pretest. Normality of change scores was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was evaluated using Levene’s test. Independent-samples t-tests were used for knowledge and attitudes when parametric assumptions were met, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was used for practices because normality was not met. A significance level of α = 0.05 was applied, using one-tailed tests corresponding to the directional hypotheses of greater improvement in the experimental group. |

# S4. Difficulty-adjustment algorithm

**State space.** Each mechanic uses four ordered difficulty levels: 1 = Easy, 2 = Medium, 3 = Hard, and 4 = Expert. A player with no prior history for a mechanic starts at level 1. After each attempt, a normalized score in [0,1] is used to select the next level.

```text
INPUT: mechanic_id, player_history
IF no previous result exists for mechanic_id:
level <- 1
ELSE:
score <- normalized score from the immediately previous attempt
level <- previous level
IF score >= 0.85:
level <- min(level + 1, 4)
ELSE IF score < 0.60:
level <- max(level - 1, 1)
ELSE:
level <- level
END IF
launch mechanic at selected level
save attempt result and normalized score
RETURN selected level
```

**Normalized mechanic scores.** The four mechanics use task-specific performance measures rescaled to a common 0–1 interval so that the same thresholds can be applied across the system. The normalized scoring rules for the four mechanics are summarized below.

| **Mechanic**           | **Normalized score definition**                                                                                                                                      |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Reception Filter       | Proportion of correct classifications relative to total classification interactions (correct vs. incorrect).                                                         |
| Critical Points Frenzy | Proportion of successfully saved thermal-control processes relative to successful plus failed/contaminated processes.                                                |
| Express Hygiene Radar  | Normalized function of correct actions and critical events, with a 0.5 penalty for each unnecessary false alarm; the final score is constrained to the 0–1 interval. |
| Crisis Board           | Proportion of dilemmas resolved in favor of the applicable safety standard relative to norm-compliant plus pressure-driven decisions.                                |

**Difficulty effects.** As level increases, visual aids are removed, environmental speed increases, and regulatory distractors become more complex. The transition rule is deterministic and bounded: scores \>= 0.85 raise difficulty by one level, scores from 0.60 to \<0.85 maintain the current level, and scores \< 0.60 reduce difficulty by one level.
